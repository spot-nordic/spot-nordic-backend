April 2026 - SMS Skin Tones v1

Just when we were getting ready to relax we found a new problem - one that we didn't know existed until recently: The problem that many brands have found when it comes to making sure that skin tones are kept consistent - even from run to run at the same print shop.

Turns out that designers/photographers and even printers don't seem to pay enough attention to skin colours of models that are used in marketing campaigns.

In a previous life I was actually responsible for making model images look their best in a professional capacity and as a colour enthusiast I of course learned everything there was to learn about this, - including that the skintone was beautiful and the teeth were white as pearls, - since yes, the job was to get jobs for those young models by only showing digital sRGB jpgs of their books to convince modeling agencies in the fashion capitals to give them a go for a few weeks infront of end customers. This is how new models are discovered nota bene. And yes, images of even top models and actors are processed this way before they are released for publication. They don't actually look this good in real life (sorry).

So, of course I decided that it would be a cool idea to develop a range of skin tones that are safe to apply digitally, if the idea is to make the skin tone of the model look fresh and attractive - v.s. blue or green or something horrible.

So I took a deep dive into what is considered a fresh and natural skin tone and decided to start with 11 skin tones for people from 11 parts of the world. It is not an absolute catalog of all people in the world but it is at least a start that photographers, designers and prepress people can use to check if the skin tone is natural - or at least in the ballpark of natural.

Here is for instance a model who has medium dark skin, so I picked SMS Africa 1o, - which is quite close to her original skin tone (based on the picture - minus some blushing in her cheeks that I killed, when I turned the image into a b/w),  the lightest tone available for African native people - according to our research.

She is nota bene wearing a dress that we coloured digitally to SMS J-11b - and a lipstick of the colour SMS J-20 (our own red colour).

Photographers and designers (that have the written permission of the photographer) that know their way around Adobe Photoshop can easily apply SMS colours to faces of models - say to apply a nice blush or unique eyeliner at their discretion, - as long as they are not advertising a proprietary line of blush or eyeliner of course. Just don't get carried away - and make sure that you use only SMS colours from the same SMS colour palette - in this case SMS Standard or SMS Standard Home & Office v7.

What I personally find super cool about these skin tones (more may be added later, - let's see) is that they are optimized to our SMS Standard Home & Office colour space, - which means that those 11 tones can actually be displayed correctly online (for monitors that only need to cover approx. 60% of the sRGB gamut/approx. 45% of the NTSC colour gamut), on Television and in CMYK printing to universal standards on both coated AND uncoated paper.

In fact the base tones fit perfectly within the colour gamut of SMS ECO, - meaning that the base tone can be printed in your local newspaper.  However, since we don't want to oversell, we are officially restricting these 11 colours to the SMS Standard Home & Office colour space.

Designers simply need to mask out the skin parts of the model, find a midtone on the face and mark it and then import the SMS skin tone patch into the document, place it next to the midtone patch and then adjust the colour of the face until the LAB value of the midtone spot and the SMS skin tone patch are the same.

NOTE: The SMS skin tones are delivered in sRGB format. If you have already converted the image to the final CMYK destination format, convert the SMS skin tone patch to that same CMYK icc profile using Absolute Colorimetric Rendering Intent, no dither, safe it as TIF(F) and then import it.

If you are already an SMS user, you will know that I sincerely despise fancy colour descriptions such as fresh automn or summer yellow - or pleasant colour.

The clear architecture of the Spot Matching System is that we define our colours by fixed 3C (Cross-media Colour Consistency) LAB values and we recommend the same for brands of any size and shape.

Make up or clothing or lipstick manufacturers that would like to use our SMS colours are welcome to do so. We are happy to teach them how to match SMS colours.  Being able to present your product in it's actual colour(s) in any relevant media should be an obvious advantage to any brand, in any category.

By the end of the day, we do not care HOW they match them, - just as long as they match them visually.  If it looks like an SMS colour, it is an SMS colour.

The SMS P20SKINo colours are available in the Spot-Nordic online store for EUR 50. Get your skin colours today.

January 2026 - uniform SMS white background colours available for a completely uniform visual experience

We have released SMS ECO and SMS Standard white backgrounds for customers that would like to maximize the visual consistency between their printed material and their website and TV graphics.

The standard white for websites is very bright as we all know, - considerably brighter than the white point of "normal" paper.

If you are an SMS user and if you want to use your paper white point for your official website - or say, something very close to the white point of the paper that you are using for your printed material, make sure that all bitmap images you use on your website are in PNG format, - preferably with the sRGB icc profile embedded to each graphic.  That way the white point of your graphics will be transparent by default and your bitmaps will not look like stamps on your website, - see example at www.spot-nordic.com, where we use a bitmap that is supposed to be severely uncoated on the left and coated on the right.

The SMS Standard White has the white point of FOGRA39/FOGRA47 - according to ISO 12647-2-2004/AMD 2007 - also very close to the white point of GRACoL CRPC6 and CRPC3 or the older GRACoL Coated 2006 and the ECO white is the target white of newspapers according to ISO 12647-3 / WAN-IFRA.

If you are using papers that have a different white point than the ones here below, you can simply measure it with a spectrophotometer and create your own white point using the LAB values you measured for optimum visual consistency between your website and your printed material.

Feel free to download the SMS whites here below. They are free of charge in sRGB, PNG format. If you need to use them for CMYK printing, use them as any other SMS colour and convert them to the destination CMYK - see instruction videos here.

March 2025 - SMS Home & Office (Standard and MAX) libraries now available

We have used this year to look into how people view colours - brand colours to be exact.

What we found out is that a large portion of people around the world are viewing colours on PC laptops that have displays that only cover approx. 45% of the NTSC colour gamut, which is approx. 60% of the sRGB gamut - not 100% - which is a requirement if you are working with the original SMS colours.

We refer to this gamut simply as the P60 gamut - named after the product number of our first laptop that has this gamut and on which the Home & Office colour palettes were built.

These laptops or notebooks are affordable and perfectly suitable for office, school and home, for building and browsing Power Point presentations and Excel charts, but not built for high end graphic work or evaluation of brand colours in general.

That again means that these people cannot see all the SMS colours that people that have better displays - 100% sRGB capable displays or Apple displays for instance. (45% of the NTSC colour gamut)

So if you happen to be marketing a certain colour, it is obviously not helpful if large portion of your prospective customers are seeing a completely different colour on their laptop displays.

This is awkward if you are marketing a brand - any brand, - but imagine for instance if you were a manufacturer of caps that you were primarily selling online in your webshop and you picked out a red colour for your line of caps, that do not look red on the displays of half of your customers - but orange.

 Would that be acceptable - for you - or for your customers?

That is why we have now launced a brand new line of SMS colours - quite similar to the standard ones - but they could be referred to as "Web Safe" colours - i.e. they typically will look quite correct on even modest office PC laptop displays.

We have named this line, which is available in the Standard and MAX range Home & Office.

For identification, the Standard v6 Home & Office colours are labelled with a small o (for office) and the MAX v6 Home & Office colours are labelled with xo at the end - x for MAX of course and the o for office.

Fortunately our original SMS ECO colours fit nicely within the small gamut of office PC laptops

Here is a screenshot from a random website of one of our Icelandic Banks - the Bank of Iceland (Islandsbanki) that we grabbed, just to demonstrate the advantage of our new Home & Office colour palettes.

The upper image is what the image is the original image, converted to sRGB by the designer.

We have inserted 2 colours from our new SMS MAX v6 Home & Office colour palette as optional red SMS colours that the designer could have used.

If your display is capable of displaying 100% sRGB, you will see that the red colours that the designer went with are quite saturated, compared to the SMS colours.

To be clear, the designers did absolutely NOTHING wrong here. They made sure that all their brand colours were within the sRGB gamut, which is the standard colour gamut used for web design globally. That is why SMS colours are adjusted to sRGB and have been from the start.

The following image has been processed by us to look as people who are viewing it on standard office (45% NTSC/60% sRGB) PC laptops will see it.

If your display is capable of displaying 100% sRGB or more, you will see that the reds in the image - the logo, the cap on the girl and the wall on the right are all of a sudden ORANGE, instead of red.

If you are looking at the images on a typical office PC laptop display, both images will be the same - and the reds more like dark orange, - not red exactly.

What happens here is that when you come across images or graphics online that are outside the gamut of your display, all colours are automatically converted to the closest colour within your display gamut.

So in this case for instance - in our opinion it would make good sense for this bank to switch to either the Home & Office SMS J-17xo for a nice cool red colour or the SMS J-17bxo for a slightly warmer - yet RED colour.

I know however for a fact that the colour of Islandsbanki is RED.

Since regretfully this happens to be the corporate colour of the Bank of Iceland, many people surely believe their colour is dark orange since that is what it looks like on their PC display.

April 2024
SMS Standard colour palette upgrade to version 6!

We are very excited to announce a major upgrade to the original Standard colour palette of the Spot Matching System.

After following and participating to an extent in discussions of experts in colour and colour management about the direction of colour and colour management with regards to ISO standards - primarily ISO 12647, we have accepted the fact that most of our users prefer to use the old Fogra 39 icc colour profile when preparing jobs for printing on coated paper, with the complementary Fogra 47L icc profile for printing on uncoated paper, following the instructions of their print shops who remain to a large extent loyal to the old standard - ISO 12647-2-2004 and have refused to make the switch to the newer ISO 12647-2-2013, - with of course a few exceptions, who offer both options - the old standard and the new one.

Experts in the field of colour management are slowly but surely accepting the fact that Fogra 51 and Fogra 52 has not been the success we all hoped it would be, when the the new ISO 12647-2-2013 standard was presented - in 2013/2014.

The world is switching to LED lighting, which contains little or no UV and printers are still measuring colours using the M0 condition, rather than the new M1 condition, even when they print on paper that contains a lot of optical brighteners - which actually means that when you visually evaluate the print outside in daylight, the colours will appear bluer/colder, than when you evaluate them indoors - due to the fact that normal daylight does contain a portion of UV, which affects paper that contains optical brighteners (OBA) this way.

The new version of the Spot Matching System, - the Standard (original) version for starters, which will be available before Drupa 2024 - and which will be included free of charge with purchases of new Techkon SpectroDens instruments during the Drupa show -  i.e. the TECHKON Color book containing the LAB values of the new SMS Standard colours, has been revised to bring the LAB value of each SMS colour considerably closer from one media to the next, - for instance when measuring SMS colours printed in CMYK on coated paper, according to Fogra 39 and the same SMS colours printed on uncoated paper according to Fogra 47L, with a dE00 of perhaps 0.2 at the most, - compared to a dE00 of up to 1 in the older versions from media to media.

As in the older version, the SMS colours are delivered to users in sRGB format, ready for conversion by the user to CMYK - optimized for absolute conversion to Fogra 39 and Fogra 47L and very well suited for conversion to Fogra 51 and Fogra 52, G7 - Gracol Coated 2006, CRPC3 and CRPC6 and Japan Color 2011 (Coated paper).  A REC 709 version is also available for Television graphics.

When we say "very well suited", what we mean is that if the colour is printed according to the respective standard, it should be impossible for any human being to see a difference between the same SMS colour printed according to Japan Color 2011 and Fogra 47L for instance.

The only visual difference, if any, in the final printed product will be due to incorrect adjustment of the printing press, incorrect ink or incorrect ink density, - not gamut problems when preparing the PDF for print to the requested CMYK standard. This was of course also true for the older versions of the system but the likeness has now improved, as close to perfection as we can, - which is of course always the goal.

True to our SMS colours, we will be switching to a new version of our own SMS logo, where we use the same colours, tweaked according to the new v6 standard, - and as can be seen, we have taken this opportunity to lighten up the K line - the "black" shades, who were not really a success among our users, while the new shades are simply the same primary full colour with a dash of black to create a dark shade of that colour.

Curious viewers can compare the colours of the old and the new logo in Photoshop. As you will see the new colours are slightly "cleaner" - less blue if you will, and of course the new "black" contains more of the full colour than the old version.

In other words, this means that users of the new version will in fact get a total of 158 cool new colours to work with, while at the same time they will have to say goodbye to the black shades from the previous version.

November 2023
The SMS Colour Wheel presented - SMS colours as mixed spot colours, SMS for teaching purposes

In the light of the fact that the Spot Matching System is not just a colour palette but a colour standard, we decided that it would be proper to release our own Colour Wheel. There have been several colour wheels through the times, all attempting to make sense of how certain colours - Primary colours, can be mixed together to make up new colours.

Art students and Graphic Design students typically play around with Primary inks to create their own colour wheel on paper.

Our colour wheel consists of Cyan, Magenta and Yellow process inks manufactured to ISO 2846-1 - the same inks that are used for CMYK offset printing due to the simple fact that the Spot Matching System is CMYK based by origin.

Since the Spot Matching System is not recipe based but LAB based, there are no "Secondary and Tertiary colours" be definition. The recipe of each colour consisting of more than one process ink depends on the substrate/paper.

It may come as a surprise to even professional offset printers, that there is no law against mixing CMYK inks together to create a spot colour. This fact was in fact what inspired me to start work on the Spot Matching System back in 2000 or so, and something that is included in my patent, issued in 2007. This also means that SMS colours can optionally be ordered from ink manufacturers as spot colours that match the CMYK process composition of any SMS colour, although they are of course typically printed in normal CMYK process printing, which is considerably cheaper than printing a separate SMS spot colour.

From my perspective a SPOT colour is not restricted to colours that are mixed to one ink, - but a colour that a brand chooses to use for it's promotion, regardless of how it is reproduced. Hence the name, the Spot Matching System.

Our CMY colour wheel is here below. It is presented in the Standard version (the original) and hence all the colours below can be reproduced in standard CMYK printing to Fogra 39, Fogra 51, Fogra 47 and Fogra 52 plus GRACoL CRPC3 and CRPC6 plus of course for web and for Television, without noticable colour shift from one media to the next.

April 3rd 2019 - SMS CMYK colours available for more neutral substrates

Until now we have simply assumed that SMS users would pick "typical, bright, white paper" for their printing jobs.

According to the current ISO 12647-2-2013 standard, typical papers are papertype 1 (bright, white, coated paper) and papertype 5 (bright, white, uncoated paper).

However, it has been pointed out that in some regions of the world, the average white point of the commercially available paper is simply much closer to the old ISO standard, - for papertypes 1,2 for coated paper and papertype 4 for uncoated paper. The white point of these papertypes is more neutral / less bluish than the current, international standard dictates.

So we decided to add SMS CMYK colour variations for these more neutral papertypes, as an option:

P9: SMS CMYK version for printing on coated paper (White point: Fogra 51, print standard Fogra 51): EUR 28 pr. colour.

P9b: SMS CMYK version for printing on coated paper (White point: Fogra 39, print standard Fogra 39): EUR 28 pr. colour*.

P10: SMS CMYK version for printing on coated paper (White point: Fogra 51, print standard Fogra 39): EUR 35 pr. colour*.

P11: SMS CMYK version for printing on uncoated paper (White point: Fogra 52, print standard Fogra 52): EUR 28 pr. colour.

P11b: SMS CMYK version for printing on uncoated paper (White point: Fogra 47, print standard Fogra 47): EUR 28 pr. colour*.

P12: SMS CMYK version for printing on uncoated paper (White point: Fogra 52, print standard Fogra 47): EUR 35 pr. colour*.

It is easy for anyone interested in this to get a spectral instrument that supports the M1 standard (D50/2°) and measure his or her papertypes to check which Fogra standard they actually belong to, - see further instructions at www.spotmatchingsystem.com/technical.

It makes perfect sense for brand owners to select papertypes for all print jobs that have similar white points.

We are happy to advice on which version of your SMS colour will be most appropriate, based on the white point of your papertype, if you provide us with simply the papertype (coated or uncoated) and the measured white point, M1 condition.
